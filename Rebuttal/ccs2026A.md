Unlike XDA and Loadstar, which require massive, architecture-specific labeled datasets for pre-training (e.g., x86 vs. ARM). It trains online directly on the target binary. This eliminates the high human and computational costs of collecting and labeling cross-architecture datasets, enabling instant adaptation to new platforms like MIPS without prior data.

# Review C
## Q1: Why did you choose to train in each iteration rather than having separate training datasets?

Iterative online training bypasses the need for large labeled datasets, whose feature distributions vary drastically across architectures. By bootstrapping off Ghidra's initial high-confidence code blocks within the target binary itself, NSDA dynamically learns the specific structural features of that exact program. This binary-specific adaptation makes it highly robust without offline pre-training, though integrating a pre-trained model remains a viable future optimization as preseted in discussion section.
